Research

Disaster risk is not just the hazard. Following the IPCC framework, my work moves through each term of the risk equation, and then asks what it takes for people to act on the result.

× × = →

Select a term to see the questions, methods and outputs behind it.

Where are landslides, floods and outburst floods likely?

I build and compare susceptibility models for data-scarce mountain districts, from terrain, rainfall, geology and land cover, and test them against thousands of recorded landslides.

A recurring finding: the choice of method and of map unit can change which places look dangerous. That is why I report uncertainty alongside every map.

Methods
AHP and Fuzzy-AHP, Google Earth Engine, ROC-AUC with bootstrap validation
Outputs
  • Garhwal landslide susceptibility, Earth Systems and Environment 2026
  • Kumaon map-unit sensitivity, submitted to Modeling Earth Systems and Environment (preprint 2026)
  • Flood susceptibility uncertainty, EGU 2026
  • GLOF susceptibility and downstream propagation, AGU26
Data
Sentinel-1 and 2, NASADEM, CHIRPS, GSI landslide inventory

Who and what sits in harm's way?

Population and infrastructure layered over the hazard maps for every subdistrict, including the highways that keep mountain districts connected.

Methods
Gridded population, road and settlement layers, zonal statistics
Outputs
Exposure component of the Uttarakhand multi-hazard risk index; highway resilience talk, National Conference on Climate Adaptive Resilient Highways (CARH 2025), National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM)

Who is least able to cope?

Exposure is not destiny. A 20-indicator social vulnerability index captures who is least able to prepare for, cope with and recover from a disaster.

Methods
Indicator selection, normalisation and weighting, census-based social data
Outputs
Social vulnerability index for 111 subdistricts of Uttarakhand

Bringing it together

A composite multi-hazard risk index for all 111 subdistricts of Uttarakhand, combining landslide, flood and GLOF susceptibility with exposure and social vulnerability. 41.4% of subdistricts fall in the high or very high risk classes.

A companion framework adds drought to rapid-onset hazards for the Indian Himalaya.

Framework
IPCC hazard, exposure and vulnerability
Outputs
  • Manuscript under review, Sustainable Development, with Alex de Sherbinin
  • Composite mapping including drought, Australia–India Water Centre (AIWC) symposium 2025, Western Sydney University

An AI research assistant for landslide science

Decades of landslide research sit in papers that practitioners rarely have time to search. With UNESCO's Disaster Risk Reduction Unit and the International Consortium on Landslides, I designed and built a retrieval-augmented assistant that answers questions from that literature and shows exactly where each answer comes from.

It is in live testing ahead of public release on the ICL website, and I present it at the 7th World Landslide Forum on 24 November 2026, with Soichiro Yasukawa, Shinji Sassa and Matjaž Mikoš.

Read how I built it

  1. 202peer-reviewed articles indexed
  2. 970+searchable passages, found by meaning rather than keywords
  3. 12languages, with every answer linked to its sources

Built with retrieval-augmented generation, vector embeddings and Cloudflare Workers.
