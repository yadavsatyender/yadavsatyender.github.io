Landslide Susceptibility Zonation and Aggregation-Scale Sensitivity Across Sub-Districts of the Kumaon Himalaya: A Comparative Analysis Using AHP and Fuzzy-AHP Framework

In brief

Hazard maps for planning are usually reported per subdistrict, so a detailed 30 m susceptibility map has to be squeezed into one value per unit. This preprint asks how much that step, the choice of summary statistic, changes which subdistricts look dangerous across all 53 subdistricts of the Kumaon Himalaya.

How it was done

  • Landslide susceptibility mapped at 30 m from nine conditioning factors with two methods, AHP and Fuzzy-AHP, compared with ROC curves and bootstrap confidence intervals.
  • The Fuzzy-AHP map summarised per subdistrict in three ways: the mean, the 90th percentile and the maximum.
  • A class-divergence map showing where the methods put the same subdistrict in different classes, checked against 3,428 validation points from the landslide inventory.

What it found

  • Averaging scored best for ranking subdistricts overall (AUC 0.786, against 0.728 for the 90th percentile).
  • But averaging under-classified small, highly susceptible zones in 24 of the 53 subdistricts (45.3%): a moderate class can hide a very dangerous slope.

Why it matters

Subdistrict maps guide budgets and building rules. Reporting an upper-tail statistic alongside the mean flags the units where field checks and stricter regulation are most needed. You can see the effect on a synthetic landscape in the map-unit demo on the home page.
