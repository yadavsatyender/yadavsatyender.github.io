Integrated Multi-Criteria Decision-Making Analysis for Sub-District Scale Landslide Susceptibility Zonation in the Garhwal Himalaya

Yadav, S., Kumar, P., Islam, M. S.

Earth Systems and Environment 10(9), 2026. Published online 2 June 2026. Published

  • Read the paper (Springer)

In brief

Monsoon rain triggers landslides across the Garhwal Himalaya every year, cutting roads and putting lives at risk, and new roads and towns add pressure on already fragile slopes. This study maps landslide susceptibility for all 58 subdistricts of Garhwal, the scale at which local authorities plan and fund mitigation.

How it was done

  • Nine factors built from satellite data and GIS: slope, aspect, elevation, geology, land use and land cover, soil erosion susceptibility, monsoon rainfall, distance to rivers and distance to roads.
  • Factor weights set with the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP), a structured way of turning expert comparisons into weights.
  • A composite index placed each subdistrict in one of five classes, from very low to very high, and the result was tested against an inventory of past landslides.

What it found

  • Slope, monsoon rainfall and geology carried the most weight, followed by distance to roads.
  • More than half of the subdistricts (55%) fall in the high or very high classes, especially in Chamoli, Rudraprayag and Uttarkashi districts.
  • Against 3,484 recorded landslides the map reached an AUC of 0.768, fair skill for a regional screening tool.

Why it matters

It gives district authorities a ranked, subdistrict-by-subdistrict view of where to put mitigation money and enforce land-use rules first. A companion study for the Kumaon Himalaya tests how much these rankings depend on how the map is averaged: see the preprint.
